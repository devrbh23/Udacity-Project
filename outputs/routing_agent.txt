Rome, Italy, has a rich and extensive history that dates back over 2,800 years. It was founded in 753 BC according to legend by Romulus and Remus, twin brothers who were raised by a she-wolf. Rome started as a small settlement on the banks of the Tiber River and grew to become the capital of the Roman Kingdom, Roman Republic, and Roman Empire.

The Roman Kingdom was established in 753 BC and lasted until 509 BC when it was overthrown and replaced by the Roman Republic. The Roman Republic was a period of great expansion and conquest, with Rome establishing control over much of the Mediterranean region. However, internal strife and power struggles led to the collapse of the Republic and the rise of the Roman Empire.

The Roman Empire was founded in 27 BC by Augustus Caesar, the first Roman Emperor. The empire reached its peak during the 2nd century AD, encompassing a vast territory that stretched from Britain to the Middle East. Rome was a center of culture, politics, and military power, with famous emperors such as Julius Caesar, Augustus, Nero, and Trajan.

The decline of the Roman Empire began in the 3rd century AD due to economic troubles, military defeats, and internal unrest. The empire was eventually split into the Western Roman Empire and the Eastern Roman Empire (Byzantine Empire) in 285 AD. The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when the Germanic chieftain Odoacer deposed the last Roman emperor, Romulus Augustulus.

Despite the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Rome remained an important city throughout the Middle Ages and Renaissance as the seat of the Papacy and a center of art, culture, and learning. Today, Rome is the capital of Italy and a major tourist destination known for its ancient ruins, art, and architecture.
